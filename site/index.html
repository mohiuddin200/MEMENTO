We act as if time is endless. We put things off, scroll a little longer, and tell ourselves we’ll start tomorrow. But life isn’t a long road. It’s a counter, and it only goes one way.

Every time you open a tab, you’ll see the days you have left and the world’s births and deaths ticking by. It reminds you that this hour won’t come back, that the scroll can wait and the work can’t.

Privacy

This section is Memento’s privacy policy. Last updated 7 October 2026.

What it stores
Your birthday, sex, country and display settings, your shortcuts, an optional background image, and a 7-day cache of World Bank figures. All of it lives in Chrome’s local storage on your device. Nothing is synced to an account or sent to us.
What it connects to
The public World Bank Open Data API (api.worldbank.org), to download life-expectancy, birth-rate, death-rate and population figures. These requests contain none of your data. To show shortcut icons, Chrome loads each shortcut site’s own favicon.
What it never does
No accounts, analytics, ads or tracking. No selling or sharing of data. No remote code: everything that runs ships inside the extension.
Deleting your data
Remove the extension and Chrome deletes everything it stored. You can also change or clear any setting from the gear icon.
Where the numbers come from
Life expectancy, crude birth and death rates and population are the World Bank’s latest figures. Live counters extrapolate those yearly rates and reset at your local midnight. Memento is approximate by design: a warning sign, not a diagnosis.
